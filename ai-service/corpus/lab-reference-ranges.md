# Laboratory reference ranges

Default ranges used by the Aurora laboratory module. Ranges are method- and
population-dependent: the hospital's own catalogue is the source of truth, and these
values only explain what the numbers usually mean.

## Complete blood count

- **Hemoglobin** — 13-17 g/dL (adults). Below range: anaemia. Above: polycythaemia or
  dehydration.
- **WBC** — 4-11 x10^3/uL. Raised with infection, inflammation, or steroids; low with
  viral illness, marrow problems, or some drugs.
- **Platelets** — 150-450 x10^3/uL. Low: bleeding risk, dengue, marrow disease. High:
  reactive thrombocytosis.
- **Hematocrit** — 40-50 %. Follows hemoglobin.

## Lipid profile

- **Total cholesterol** — desirable below 200 mg/dL.
- **LDL** — below 100 mg/dL is optimal; 130-159 is borderline high.
- **HDL** — above 40 mg/dL is protective.
- **Triglycerides** — below 150 mg/dL.

## Glycaemia

- **HbA1c** — 4.0-5.7 % normal; 5.7-6.4 % prediabetes; 6.5 % and above diabetes range.

## Liver

- **ALT** 7-56 U/L, **AST** 10-40 U/L — raised in hepatitis, fatty liver, and drug injury.
- **Bilirubin** 0.1-1.2 mg/dL — raised in cholestasis or haemolysis; visible jaundice
  usually above 2.5 mg/dL.

## Urinalysis

- **Protein** — negative or trace. Persistent protein suggests renal disease.
- **Glucose** — negative; positive suggests hyperglycaemia.
- **RBC** — 0-2 per high-power field; more suggests infection, stones, or trauma.

## How Aurora flags results

Aurora stores a low and a high reference value per parameter. When a result is entered
the platform marks it **low**, **normal**, or **high** automatically, and the report
shows the reference range next to every value. A doctor reviews the report, adds an
interpretation, and releases it to the patient.
